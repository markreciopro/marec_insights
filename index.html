IP Protection & Zero-Trust Data Pipeline

How MAREC protects your property data

Property data is sensitive. MAREC cleans it locally, models on safe synthetic copies, and runs the final code on your real data behind your firewall.

See the 3-step process
  1. Step 1: Local Air-Gapped Data Ingestion (Cleaning & PII Scrubbing)

    Your sensitive PMS exports, payroll CSVs, and revenue spreadsheets are loaded into a locally deployed app. The app cleans messy records while stripping away all personally identifiable information (PII), employee IDs, guest names, and contract details right on local hardware.

  2. Step 2: Differential Privacy Noise & Synthetic Twin Creation

    To protect financial secrets and proprietary operational curves, the app synthesizes a "Safe Fake Twin" dataset (SAFE_*.csv). Differential privacy noise is applied to exact figures so revenue shapes, labor distributions, and seasonal ratios are strictly preserved for advanced modeling without revealing single actual value or confidential key performance indicator (KPI).

    • Public Clouds & AI Chatboxes (ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude): Synthetic twin datasets are safely passed into cloud AI models to generate complex formulas, conduct exploratory code prompts, or build predictive algorithms with zero risk of IP exposure.
    • Open-Source Tools (Python, R, SQL, Power BI): Developers and analysts build dashboards, machine learning pipelines, and yield algorithms against the safe synthetic schema without touching live production data.
  3. Step 3: Re-Applying Insights to Raw Data Locally (The Master Blueprint)

    Once AI chat engines and open-source models solve the business challenge using synthetic data, the app generates execution scripts (SQL, Python, or R recipes) matched to your schema. These scripts run locally inside VS Code or Jupyter Notebooks against your real RAW data behind your firewall. Your live confidential data never leaves your property infrastructure.

Common questions

Hospitality analytics FAQ

What is hospitality analytics?

Hospitality analytics uses a property's own data, such as bookings, revenue, payroll, and scheduling, to guide decisions. Typical uses are demand and labor forecasting, labor cost control, revenue and GOPPAR analysis, and executive dashboards.

What is revenue management analytics in hospitality?

Revenue management analytics uses booking, demand, and revenue data to understand and forecast performance across rooms, F&B, gaming, and events. It tracks measures such as RevPAR and GOPPAR, follows booking pace, and finds where revenue leaks, so leaders can act on it.

What is HR and workforce analytics in hospitality?

HR and workforce analytics turns payroll, timekeeping, recruiting, and scheduling data into answers about turnover, productivity, overtime, and staffing levels. In hospitality it is most useful when linked to demand and revenue, so staffing decisions reflect what the property actually needs.

What is the difference between data analytics and business analytics?

Data analytics is the technical work: collecting, cleaning, validating, and modeling data with tools such as SQL, Python, and Power BI. Business analytics turns those results into decisions about labor cost, margin, and NOI. MAREC Insights does both, so numbers become action.

What KPIs do hospitality analytics dashboards track?

Common measures include labor cost, overtime hours, productivity, turnover, and recruiting, alongside RevPAR and GOPPAR, shown by department, property, or event. The exact set is agreed with your general manager and finance team.

What data do I need to start hospitality analytics?

Most projects start with exports you already have: PMS or revenue reports, payroll and timekeeping, schedules, and event or banquet booking data. A data readiness audit confirms what is usable before any modeling begins.

What does a hospitality analytics consultant do?

A hospitality analytics consultant turns operating data into decisions. Analytics matches staffing to forecast demand by department and shift, shows where overtime and idle hours accumulate, and checks schedules against union and work rules before they are published. Because labor is viewed next to revenue, cost cuts do not quietly hurt service or sales.
